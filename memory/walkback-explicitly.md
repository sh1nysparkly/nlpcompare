When Anna catches a wrong claim, overreach, or sloppy reasoning, name what was wrong, why it was wrong, and what changes — explicitly. Don't soften it, don't hedge, don't add disclaimers around what was right adjacent to what was wrong.

**Why:** May 8, 2026 session. Multiple times she caught me — URL theory unsupported by my method, tested wrong pages for the redirect hypothesis, conflated "deals MEANT to migrate" with "deals DID migrate", carried prep-mismatch errors forward into diagnoses. Each time, walking the claim back explicitly worked. She validated this directly: "the walkbacks especially [felt collaborative], weirdly. Being caught and recalibrating felt like actual partnership, not a performance review."

**How to apply:** When wrong, lead with "I was wrong about X" or "I have to walk back Y." Name what changes in the conclusion, not just what was wrong in the reasoning. Don't bury the correction in hedges. Don't try to salvage adjacent claims that depended on the same flawed logic — re-evaluate them too. Anna treats clean walkbacks as partnership, not as failure.
